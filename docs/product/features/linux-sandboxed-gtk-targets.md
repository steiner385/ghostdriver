Ghostdriver can identify, read, and safely drive Flatpak-sandboxed GTK/wx applications on Linux over the AT-SPI bus — within the part of the accessibility tree that is actually reachable, and without taking the desktop from the operator.

## Problem

A Flatpak-sandboxed GTK/wx application breaks five assumptions the Linux backend made for ordinary targets, and it breaks them at five different *layers of the stack* rather than in any one program:

- **The sandbox:** its X windows carry no `_NET_WM_PID`, and AT-SPI reports the `xdg-dbus-proxy` pid rather than the application's real pid.
- **wxGTK3:** its fields don't commit on a display-only write; committing needs a real Enter keystroke — tier-4 live actuation.
- **GTK on X11:** `grab_focus` steals the X foreground as a side effect of focusing a field.
- **Owner-drawn widgets:** custom-painted toolbar and sidebar controls aren't exposed on the a11y bus at all — empty names, `INT_MIN` extents.
- **mutter:** it refuses non-interactive activation, so scripted un-minimizing or raising is not a fallback.

Bambu Studio 02.08 is the **specimen these were measured on** — chosen because it breaks all five at once — not the subject of the capability. Nothing under `src/` branches on it: the product name appears only in docstrings recording which measurement forced the generic code, and `application_name(pid)` asks the bus what it calls an application rather than knowing any application by name. Before this cluster of measurements, a target like this was filed as one the strategy couldn't fit (D53) rather than scoped and driven. Issues #101, #102, #103, #110, #111, #115, #116 are the connected findings and decisions.

## Target users

- Operators driving a sandboxed GTK/wx application on their own Linux desktop through Ghostdriver, who need the automation to never take the X foreground away from them mid-task.
- Ghostdriver maintainers extending the Linux backend to further Flatpak GTK/wx targets, who need the pid-identity and live-actuation patterns established here as precedent rather than re-derived per application.

## Desired outcome

`list-targets` and `attach` resolve a sandboxed target to one consistent identity despite its three-way identity split (real pid vs. a11y proxy pid vs. X window). Reading through the a11y bus works unconditionally. Committing a value change — which on wxGTK3 requires a real Enter — is possible only under an explicit, narrowly scoped "operator-present live actuation" consent grant that aborts the moment the foreground or pointer changes. A write to an unnamed field is addressed by place *and* by what it shows, and confirmed by comparison rather than by reading the value back (D#105), so a wrong-field write is impossible rather than merely unlikely, and nothing the application displays reaches a result, a trace or a model's context. The backend's documented contract matches what the bus can actually reach: `EditableText` fields only, located by position and current value; owner-drawn controls are screenshot-only and out of scope for the bus.

## Non-goals

- Driving Bambu Studio's owner-drawn toolbar/sidebar controls (Slice/Print buttons, parameter labels) through the a11y bus — out of scope by measurement (#115, #116); only screenshot-plus-coordinate-click applies there. That separate capability now has driving items on both platforms, not just this one (#141, #63 on the Windows side), which is the argument for filing it as its own entry rather than growing this one. The Windows side has since hit the same wall from the other direction: D#1 ran the three unprobed routes against a real owner-drawn grid — Windows' own `charmap.exe`, `CharGridWClass` — and all three came back negative, closing #1 and #3. That corroborates this non-goal across two toolkits; it does not narrow it.
- Claiming the stack is *solved* on the strength of one specimen. This capability is **scoped** to the stack, but its evidence base is a single application: the pid-identity bridge (#101, #110) and the tier-4 commit (#102, #111) were measured against Bambu Studio and stand as precedent, not as a guarantee they fit other Flatpak GTK/wx targets unchanged. Naming the capability after the stack is a statement of scope, not of coverage — a second specimen is how that gap closes, and until one is measured, "works on sandboxed GTK/wx" means "worked on the one we measured."
- Growing application-specific knowledge to make a target work. No code under `src/` branches on an application's identity and none should (D59 keeps everything above `backends/` platform-neutral; `tests/test_structure.py` enforces the boundary). A target the stack cannot fit is reported as such, not special-cased (D53).
- Replacing the production tuning path for this printer (`x2d-pro/bambu-studio/tune.py` + `verify.py`); this backend is a research/automation track alongside that path, not a replacement for it.

## Notes

**How this capability holds the non-disruption line, and the one place it bends.** Three of the four rungs are genuinely separate from the operator: reading the bus, display-only writes, and posted input never touch the foreground, and `tests/test_zz_focus_guard.py` fails any run in which a test-spawned window takes it. The measurement that mattered most here was a *refusal* — D#102 found `grab_focus` works on wxGTK3 and steals the X foreground, and D#111 gated it rather than shipping it.

Tier 4 is the exception, and it is cooperative rather than isolated. A wxGTK3 field commits only on a real Enter, so that rung uses the operator's actual keyboard on their actual display; it is bounded by an explicit per-session consent grant, a check that the named window is already the operator's live foreground, and an abort on any pointer or focus change, reported as a disturbance (D28) rather than a verdict. D#124 states the principle plainly from the Windows side: D9 excludes real input by default "because that is the exact interference this project exists to avoid," and D#130 requires the cheaper route be tried first. So "non-disruptive" here means *isolated where possible, consented and abortable where the toolkit leaves no other route* — not that tier 4 is invisible to the person at the desk.

**Most of this cluster has landed.** PR #113 merged the live wxGTK3 slice in one piece, which is why the driving issues read as unbuilt while the code is present: D#101/D#110 (Flatpak pid identity — XRes-resolved real pid, a11y proxy pid, and `application_name` aliasing, in `backends/linux/tree.py` and `backends/linux/display.py`), D#102/D#111 (the operator-present live actuation gate — the tool-layer half in `tools/live.py`, the tier-4 commit and its abort conditions in `backends/linux/live.py`, folding #103), and the two that arrived with it, D#104 (the element view as a read of the enumeration registry, `backends/linux/elements.py`) and D#105 (the place-plus-value locator and `confirms_value`, in `model/locator.py` and `resolve.py`).

**Issues #101, #102, #103, #110 and #111 are still open even though their decisions are recorded and implemented** — treat the decision record, not the issue state, as the signal here, and check `docs/DECISIONS.md` before scheduling any of them as work.

**What is genuinely outstanding**, as of 2026-09-18: the `EditableText`-only scope decision (#115) remains an unmerged *draft* PR #116 (`claude/linux-backend-scope`, adds `docs/linux-backend-scope.md`, last touched 2026-09-11) — so the metric above that names that file is not yet met.

- Why "located by position *and* current value" is not a contradiction of the withholding policy (D#34b): the value half is matched by *comparison*, never by disclosure. `_current_value` (`src/ghostdriver/backends/linux/elements.py`) returns `""` for any editable entry, because AT-SPI cannot tell a plain `Gtk.Entry` from a password field — so a `value=` locator is resolved through a `confirm(node, expected) -> bool | None` callback whose contents never reach the resolver, a result, a trace or a model's context (D#105, D#34b). The backend half of that comparison is `confirms_value` in `src/ghostdriver/backends/linux/reading.py`. A consequence worth stating in the map, because it is the behaviour an operator meets first: a `value=` locator supplied *without* a `confirm` is **refused**, not matched loosely (`_value_matches`, `src/ghostdriver/resolve.py`) — the D33 rule applied to withheld content, where the resolver cannot see what it is being asked to match.
